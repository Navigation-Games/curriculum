                     * Spatial Descriptions

                     * Medium: Find the animal farthest from the Blue cone.
                     * Hard: Find all the animals between the Yellow and Green cone.


(If you have enough time and the group is not too advanced, include the following)

(Read this final activity step regardless of if you did the circle the checkpoint activity above)

Barb Notes
                     * Concepts
                     * Which features to put on the map; map symbols
                     * Landmarks (subset of features that are easily recognizable in real life and on the map)
                     * Spatial relationship vocabulary - being able to describe the location of one cone relative to landmarks and other cones - being able to describe groupings of cones/features
                     * Orienting the map, and keeping it oriented as you do the course
                     * Using landmarks
                     * Using spatial relationships of features
                     * Course: start triangle, finish double circle, checkpoints are in the CENTER of each circle [I recently had a middle school student doing the maze who was trying to go to the number instead of the circle], doing it in the order shown.
                     *                      * Teaching “tricks”
                     * Definitely have them do Animal-O first, so they get familiar with the locations of the cones, and so that you can have an activity that requires little explanation to get them moving.
                     * I think the “spatial descriptions” section in the long form lesson plan might belong before the “whiteboard” section.
                     * There are various ways to introduce the map, depending on how much time you have and the students’ level, and their patience for talking and discussion. You could draw it in front of them, while discussing which features to put on the map. Or, you could show them an already-drawn map as in the lesson plan.
