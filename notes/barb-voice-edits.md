# Barb's edits to course text

A running log of Barb's edits and comments on the teacher course text, so the patterns can go into the voice guidance (the "Writing in Barb's Voice" section of CLAUDE.md and the Voice section of `ai-advisor/tutor-prompt.md`). Add new edits at the bottom with the date and where they were made. When a pattern shows up more than once, promote it to the guidance.

## October 26, 2026: Unit 1 (What Orienteering Is and Why It Works)

| Before | After / comment | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "Orienteering is a sport where you use a map to visit checkpoints, and how you get from one to the next is up to you, because there's no marked route." | Full stop after "visit checkpoints"; new sentence starting "How you get..." | Split a long joined sentence at its natural break. |
| "That's what makes it interesting for kids: they decide where to go, pick a way to get there, and notice when they've gone wrong." | Removed. | Cut sentences that sell or explain why something is interesting; let the content speak. |
| "[About Orienteering] has the longer version." | Ask them to read it. | Give a direct request instead of mentioning that something exists. |
| (no video) | Show the IOF video, labeled as competitive orienteering, with credit to the International Orienteering Federation. | Show the real thing, and credit sources by full name. |
| The three ideas (checking, growth mindset, being lost) | "Sound a little random, but I still like them." Wants to reword the sentences after them (coming back to this). | Explain why a set of ideas belongs together, or the list feels arbitrary. |
| "What we look for" rubric | "I do really like the rubric." | Short, concrete, checkable criteria work. |
| Tutor: "after reading the page" | Be specific: which page? | Name the exact page or thing, never "the page". |
| Tutor: ask who the pitch is for only at the role-play | Ask first who the listener is, since the benefit depends on it. | Establish the audience before asking for content written for it. |
| Checking bullet: "Confirm before you move on... This is also how the lessons run without electronic timing." | Rewritten around what students do (match a picture or code, partner first, then alone) plus Barb's own sentence linking it to competition: "In competition, electronic timing is used to confirm the athletes visited the correct checkpoints in the correct order, but the athletes first check themselves at each checkpoint with a 'clue sheet'." Chose the shorter of two drafts. | Prefers the shorter draft. Connects what students learn to what real orienteers do. Keeps her own concrete sentence over a smoother paraphrase. |
