Junction Control

Teams race to visit every checkpoint, traveling only on trails. The team with the most players at an intersection blocks it, and players who arrive outnumbered are held until the blocking team lets them go. When a team has every checkpoint, everyone heads for a bus stop on the edge of the map. The first team with everyone on a bus wins.

    How the simulator plays

    The short version is below. The help page explains every setting, each way of playing, and what the simulator has shown so far.

    • An intersection is a place with at least three ways to leave it. Bends and dead ends get no number, and nobody blocks or is held there.
    • Players run 2.5 to 3.4 meters a second, and pairs and packs move at their slowest member's speed. Each group heads for the nearest checkpoint no other group on its team is going for. When none is left, it backs up a teammate by heading for the nearest one anyway.
    • A rescue is a swap: when one more teammate would free a held player, the nearest free teammate within 350 meters comes. The held player leaves first, and the rescuer stays in their place (unless the rule frees both).
    • Blockers leave their intersection when every remaining checkpoint (two at most) has a teammate headed for it, timed to catch a bus no later than the one the last of those teammates will catch.
    • "Players see a block" sets what players know. From anywhere, they route around every block. Otherwise they find out when they get close, may turn back, and remember what they saw for 3 minutes.
    • Not in the simulator: talk between players or teams, deals, the game operator's announcements, players leaving the trails, or anyone staying lost for longer than one wrong turn.
