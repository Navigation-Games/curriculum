# More Games

These games aren't part of the lesson sequences. They're great for a special day, a field day station, a camp evening, or a group that has done the core activities and wants something new.

## Maze-O

*"You will be finding your way through a maze today."*

Students navigate through a small maze to visit checkpoints in order. To find the right checkpoint, they have to connect the walls and paths of the maze to the walls and paths on their map. If they lose that connection, they run into a dead end or an unexpected turn, and that's how they learn. Kids love it, and it works from about first grade up, depending on the size of the maze.

**Time and space:** 30-60 minutes. A gym, schoolyard, or park with a flat area about 20 by 20 feet for a small maze.

**Goals:** keeping the map oriented, route choice, planning ahead, speed and agility.

**Vocabulary:** [orient the map](/reference/glossary#orient-the-map) (turn the map so it matches the maze around you), [route choice](/reference/glossary#route-choice) (the path you choose from one checkpoint to the next).

### Materials

- Something to make the walls: chalk or tape on a hard surface, or stakes and string or flagging tape on grass
- Cones for checkpoints, each with a letter or a punch
- Maze maps, one per course, with the start, finish, and checkpoints marked
- An answer key for each course

### Setup

1. Build the maze. NG's usual maze is a 7 by 7 grid, and one person can set it up in about 30 minutes (faster with chalk, slower with stakes and string). Leave at least 1 meter between walls so two people can pass each other.
2. Print a map for each course and label it. Make an answer key that lists the checkpoint letters in order for each course.
3. Place the checkpoint cones. Put the start and finish outside the maze and mark them on the map.

### How to run it

1. **Explain the challenge.** Visit the checkpoints in the order shown on your map, then come back to the finish as fast as you can. There is absolutely no jumping over or ducking under the walls. That's for fair play and for safety.
2. **Hand out maps.** It's fine for students to look at their map before they start, and for different students to have different courses.
3. **Orient the maps.** Make sure everyone can turn their map to match the maze before anyone goes in.
4. **Go.** Students can go alone or in pairs. At each checkpoint they write down the letter or punch their card.
5. **Check at the finish.** Compare their letters to the answer key. If something is wrong, tell them which checkpoint, ask them to figure out where the right one is, and let them run the course again.
6. **Try a faster way.** After a few courses, have them rerun one and look for a different path between checkpoints. Can they beat their time? Students who are done can help someone who is stuck.

Sample script: "Find all the checkpoints on your map, in order, by running through the maze. No jumping over or ducking under the walls. The key is keeping your map turned to match the maze and making a plan for how to get to the next checkpoint. It's okay to make mistakes at first. That's how we get better!"

### Reflection

- Were some paths between checkpoints faster than others? Why?
- Was it ever hard to keep your map facing the right way? What made it hard?
- Was a longer path ever faster? How could that happen?

### Variations

- **Big Maze.** A bigger maze is more fun and more challenging. Plan carefully: a 10 by 10 grid is double the size of a 7 by 7 and takes much longer to set up. Get a helper for anything bigger than 7 by 7.
- **Maze Runner.** Run the whole course without stopping at the checkpoints. Students have to read ahead and plan the next checkpoint before they reach the current one.
- **Memory Maze.** Memorize all the checkpoints before going into the maze, and leave the map at the start.
- **Corn Maze.** A half-day or full-day field trip for students with strong navigation skills. You can't see over the walls, so students have to stay in touch with the map the whole time, which makes it great practice for a real orienteering map. Contact the corn maze owner well in advance for permission. You may have to make the map yourself, so ask the owner whether they have an aerial photo.
