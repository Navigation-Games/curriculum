## Goals

### Orienteering Goals
- Identify the boundary of the play area
- Tell whether landmarks and locations are inside or outside the boundary
- Know where home base is from anywhere in the boundary
- Respond quickly to the gathering signal
- Explain why boundaries matter for safety

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Perform locomotor skills (S1.E1)
- Move in general space safely (S1.E2)
- Follow rules and instructions (S4.E1)
- Engage actively in class (S3.E2)

## Delivery

1. **Walk the boundary** together as a class. Point out landmarks along the way: "This is the fence. This is the big tree. This is the sidewalk."
2. **Run the boundary.** Now try it at a jog. Stay together.
3. Quiz: point to landmarks and ask "inside or outside?"
4. Talk about off-limits areas within the boundary (gardens, parking lots, puddles).
5. Set up home base with a flag or cone. Students walk to different spots. "Can you point to home base from where you are?"
6. [**Gathering**](/activities/core/gathering): students move freely within the boundary. On the signal, everyone returns to home base. Count together: how many seconds did it take?
7. Repeat the gathering. Can we beat our time? Spread out a little further each round.

## Reflection

- What does boundary mean?
- How can crossing the boundary be dangerous?
- What are some things that mark boundaries? (Fences, curbs, signs)
- How did you know where home base was?
- Was it harder to come back from far away? What helped?

## Extensions

- Travel the boundary with different movements (skipping, galloping, tiptoe, giant steps)
- **Follow the Leader**: teacher leads the boundary with silly walks; students copy
- **Freeze Tag**: play freeze tag inside the boundary; if you step outside, you're frozen
- Use a different gathering signal (wave a flag, clap a pattern)
- Move home base to a new location; can you still find it?
