# Electronic Timing

Navigation Games often uses an electronic timing system with its orienteering activities. You do not need electronics to teach orienteering! Every lesson in this curriculum works without it. We use it because it helps us make sure students understand the activity, because kids love the beeping, and because it frees us from checking results so we can focus on teaching.

The timing system does two jobs:

1. **Checking** that each student visited all the checkpoints in the right order
2. **Timing** how long the course took

Both can be done without electronics. Students can check themselves by matching a picture or code at each checkpoint to their clue sheet, or a partner can check them (see [Checking](/reference/frameworks/skill-concept-sequence/#checking) and [Checkpoints](/reference/equipment/controls-and-punches)). A stopwatch or a class timer handles the timing.

## SPORTident and EasyGecNG

[SPORTident](https://www.sportident.com/) is the electronic timing system used at most orienteering events around the world. Each student carries an **SI card**, and each checkpoint has an **SI box**. Students check in by putting the SI card into the hole in the box. The box beeps and flashes and the card records the visit.

**EasyGecNG** is the program that reads the SI cards at the end of a course. It tells each student whether they visited the checkpoints in the right order and shows their time. A smiley face means the course was done correctly. Navigation Games made EasyGecNG from EasyGec, a program by Thierry Porret, to give students easier-to-read feedback about which checkpoints they missed. Intern Charlie Moore [wrote about why we changed it](https://www.navigationgames.org/post/in-the-works-new-software-for-teaching-orienteering). Huge thanks to T. Porret for sharing his code and letting us distribute it as open source.

## What students do

1. **Clear.** Check in at the Clear box. This erases the SI card from the last run.
2. **Start.** Check in at the Start box. The time starts.
3. **Checkpoints.** Visit each checkpoint in order and check in at its SI box. Hold the SI card in the box until it beeps.
4. **Finish.** Check in at the Finish box. The time stops.
5. **Download.** Put the SI card in the mini reader at the computer. EasyGecNG shows whether you got it right, and your time.

If a box doesn't beep, the card probably wasn't cleared. Go back to the Clear box.

## Timing individuals and timing the whole class

Orienteering is normally a timed sport, like cross country running or skiing. Timing students is a great way to encourage them to build speed, improve their skills and even practice their memory, as in Animal-O, where students repeat a course faster and then from memory. It also gives competition to students who want it. Not every student is comfortable being timed, though, especially while they're still learning a new activity. Even when you time students, emphasize accuracy over raw speed. Finding all the right checkpoints matters more than finding them quickly.

You can also time the whole class against one clock. This unites the students and builds teamwork. It sets the expectation that everyone is working together and that each person's actions affect the group, and it encourages students to help each other learn.
