# Video Script: Score-O

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers and program leaders

## Opening (visual: title card)

Score-O is the first activity where students choose their own route. They get a map with checkpoint locations marked, and their job is to visit as many as they can within a time limit, in any order. It is the bridge between structured courses and real orienteering.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Orienteering maps with checkpoint circles pre-printed (one per student or pair)
- Checkpoint markers: cones or flags at each location, each with a visible letter code
- A all-checkpoints map showing all checkpoint placements
- Scorecards and pencils (one per student)
- A whistle for the start and finish signal

[SHOW: close-up of a map with circles marked, a checkpoint marker with a letter code, a scorecard, and a pencil]

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen as bullet list)

- Students plan a route through multiple checkpoints, choosing which to visit and in what order
- Students navigate independently using an orienteering map to find checkpoints
- Students read an orienteering map and match symbols to real features while moving
- Students develop strategies for visiting the most checkpoints in the time allowed

## Setup (visual: aerial/wide shot of the space being set up)

1. Place checkpoint markers at locations shown on the all-checkpoints map. Each checkpoint should have a visible letter code.
[SHOW: teacher placing a cone with a letter card at a feature on the map]

2. Spread checkpoints across the mapped area at varying distances from the start. Place a few easy ones close to the start so every student finds at least one.
[SHOW: aerial view of the area with checkpoints placed, some near the start, some far]

3. Mark a clear start/finish location.
[SHOW: a visible start/finish cone or banner]

4. Print maps with checkpoint circles already marked. Students should not have to draw circles on their own.
[SHOW: stack of printed maps with circles visible]

## How to Run It (visual: demonstration with students)

1. Gather students with their maps and scorecards. Orient the map together.
[SHOW: group at start, teacher holding up the map and orienting it]

2. Point out a few checkpoint circles on the map. Ask: "What feature is this checkpoint near?" Get students reading the map, not just looking at circles.
[SHOW: teacher pointing to a circle on the map, student answering "the path junction"]

3. Explain the rules: visit checkpoints in any order. At each one, find the letter code and write it on your scorecard next to the checkpoint number. Only checkpoints with the correct letter count.
[SHOW: close-up of student writing a letter on a scorecard at a checkpoint]

4. Before starting, discuss strategy: "If you want to visit the most checkpoints, what would your plan be?" Let students share ideas. Highlight useful strategies: start with nearby ones, visit clusters together, avoid backtracking.
[SHOW: students pointing at their maps and discussing routes]

5. Set the time limit. Eight minutes for beginners. Announce it clearly.
[SHOW: teacher announcing "You have 8 minutes. When you hear the whistle, come back to the start."]

6. Start. Students go individually or in pairs.
[SHOW: students running out from the start in different directions]

7. At the time limit, blow the whistle. Students return to the start.
[SHOW: teacher blowing whistle, students running back]

8. Score: count the correct letter codes on each scorecard.
[SHOW: teacher and student checking a scorecard together]

## Progression (visual: side-by-side comparison of basic vs advanced)

- **First time**: pairs, short time limit (8 minutes), fewer checkpoints clustered in a smaller area.
[SHOW: pair of students navigating together in a compact area]

- **Experienced**: individual, longer time limit (10-12 minutes), more checkpoints spread further apart.
[SHOW: single student navigating a larger area]

- **Point values**: assign different point values to harder-to-find checkpoints. Ones that are further away or require more complex navigation are worth more.
[SHOW: map with point values written next to checkpoint circles]

- **Competitive**: compare scores. Use fastest time as tiebreaker for students with the same number of checkpoints.
[SHOW: whiteboard with names, scores, and times listed]

- **Second round**: same map, same checkpoints. Students apply what they learned in round one. Scores almost always go up.
[SHOW: student studying their map between rounds, planning a better route]

## Common Mistakes (visual: split screen correct vs incorrect)

1. **Setting the time limit too long.** Keep the first round short: 8 minutes. Students can always do a second round. A long time limit leads to wandering without purpose. A short one creates urgency and focus.
[SHOW: left side, student wandering aimlessly after 15 minutes. Right side, student moving purposefully with 8 minutes on the clock]

2. **No late penalty.** Without a consequence for returning late, some students will ignore the whistle and keep collecting checkpoints. Deduct 1 point per minute late. Announce this rule before starting.
[SHOW: left side, students trickling back 5 minutes after the whistle. Right side, teacher explaining the rule, students hustling back on time]

3. **Students running without reading the map.** Watch for students who sprint in random directions instead of stopping to orient. Pull them aside and remind them: orient the map first, then pick your next checkpoint, then move.
[SHOW: left side, student running randomly. Right side, student stopping, orienting the map, then running with purpose]

## Wrap-up (visual: return to presenter)

Score-O gives students their first taste of real orienteering: they read the map, plan a route, and make their own decisions. Keep the first round short, require correct letter codes, and always do a strategy discussion before you start. Once students can navigate independently in a Score-O, they are orienteers.
