# Video Script: Animal-O

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers and program leaders

## Opening (visual: title card)

Animal-O is the first real orienteering activity your students will do. They use a clue sheet to find animal checkpoints in order, building spatial memory and observation skills. It works in a gym, a schoolyard, or a park, and students love it.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints: cones with animal pictures attached (laminated pictures work best)
- Clue sheets: strips showing sequences of animal pictures (prepare several different sequences)
- A start/finish marker
- A defined boundary (from the Boundary Run)
- Optional: a whiteboard near start/finish to track times

[SHOW: close-up of animal pictures on cones, then a fan of different clue sheet strips]

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen as bullet list)

- Students use a clue sheet to complete an orienteering course in order
- Students check the animal picture at each checkpoint to confirm they have the right one
- Students develop spatial memory by repeating courses and tracking where checkpoints are
- Students manage quick changes of direction while moving through the space

## Setup (visual: aerial/wide shot of the space being set up)

1. Place animal-picture cones around the play area, spread out within the boundary. Use 8-12 checkpoints.
[SHOW: teacher placing cones with animal pictures, spaced across a field]

2. Set up a start/finish marker.
[SHOW: a cone or flag at the designated start area]

3. Prepare clue sheets with different sequences. The kit includes six 5-animal clue sheets and 10-animal clue sheets for a challenge.
[SHOW: close-up of 5-animal clue sheet next to a 10-animal one]

4. Optional: set up a whiteboard near the start to track completions or times.
[SHOW: whiteboard with columns for names and times]

## How to Run It (visual: demonstration with students)

1. Before handing out clue sheets, walk the group to a few checkpoints together. At each one, ask: "Which animal is this?"
[SHOW: group walking to a cone, student identifying the animal]

2. Back at the start, hand out clue sheets. Hold one up and explain: "Find all the animals in order, top to bottom. At each checkpoint, check that the picture on the cone matches your clue sheet. Then move to the next one."
[SHOW: teacher holding up clue sheet, pointing to the first animal]

3. Students go individually or in pairs. We recommend pairs for younger students.
[SHOW: pairs of students running out from the start]

4. Students find the first animal on their clue sheet, run to that checkpoint, confirm the animal matches, then continue to the next.
[SHOW: student arriving at a cone, comparing the picture to their clue sheet, then running to the next]

5. After completing the last checkpoint, students return to the finish.
[SHOW: student running back to the start area]

6. When a student finishes, give them the option to repeat the same course faster, try it from memory, or pick up a different clue sheet.
[SHOW: student returning clue sheet and grabbing a new one]

## Progression (visual: side-by-side comparison of basic vs advanced)

- **5-animal clue sheet**: learn the format with a standard course. Good for first-timers.
[SHOW: student with a 5-animal strip, visiting cones in order]

- **Repeat for speed**: same course, beat your previous time. This is where spatial memory kicks in.
[SHOW: student running the same course confidently, faster]

- **From memory**: put the clue sheet away and try to complete the course. Students who have done it twice can usually do this.
[SHOW: student handing clue sheet to teacher, then running the course without it]

- **Different sheet**: a new sequence so memorized routes do not help. Resets the challenge. There are six different 5-animal courses in the kit.
[SHOW: student picking up a new clue sheet with different animals]

- **10-animal clue sheet**: longer courses covering more of the space, for pairs who finish all six 5-animal courses.
[SHOW: student with a longer strip, moving across a wider area]

- **Solo**: if students started in pairs, try it individually.
[SHOW: single student navigating independently]

- **Animal Relay**: in small teams, students take turns running to a named animal checkpoint and back. A good closer once students know the checkpoint locations.
[SHOW: teams lined up, a runner returning and tagging the next runner]

## Common Mistakes (visual: split screen correct vs incorrect)

1. **Students running to the nearest cone without checking their clue sheet.** They need to find the right animal, not just the closest checkpoint. If this is happening, pause and remind them to check the picture at every cone.
[SHOW: left side, student running to the nearest cone without looking at their sheet. Right side, student stopping at a cone and comparing the picture]

2. **Skipping Explore & Find.** If students have never seen the checkpoints, jumping straight to clue sheets can be overwhelming. Run the Explore & Find readiness activity first, or at least walk the group to a few checkpoints so they know what they are looking for.
[SHOW: left side, confused students with clue sheets. Right side, teacher walking the group past a few cones first]

3. **Never moving checkpoints.** If checkpoints stay in the same spots every class, students memorize locations instead of building navigation skills. Move them to new locations periodically to reset the challenge.
[SHOW: left side, student running a memorized route without looking at the clue sheet. Right side, teacher relocating cones before class]

## Wrap-up (visual: return to presenter)

Animal-O is the activity students ask to repeat. The built-in differentiation, from 5-animal clue sheets to memory courses, means every student can work at their own level. Once they are succeeding with all six 5-animal courses, they are ready for Geometric-O and their first maps.
