# Video Script: Lesson 1 - Boundary

**Duration target:** 4-5 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

This is Lesson 1 in the orienteering progression. Students learn what a boundary is, how to identify it, and why it matters for safety and respect. This lesson lays the foundation for every lesson that follows.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Cones (optional, for marking edges that aren't obvious)
- A gym, schoolyard, or local park with a clear perimeter
- Walk the space beforehand and identify the boundary. Place cones if edges are unclear.

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Identify the boundary of the play area
- Tell whether landmarks and locations are inside or outside the boundary
- Recognize areas within the boundary that are still off limits
- Explain why boundaries matter for safety and respect

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Demonstrate locomotor skills (S1.E1, S1.E2)
- Follow rules and instructions (S4.E1)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

This is a short lesson, about 15 to 30 minutes. Plan for 5 minutes of Boundary Run, 5 minutes of the inside/outside quiz, 5 minutes of discussion, and any remaining time for extensions.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Boundary Run (5 min)

[SHOW: teacher jogging with a group of students along the edge of the play area]

Start with the Boundary Run. Jog the entire perimeter of the space together as a group. As you go, point out key landmarks: the big tree, the fence corner, the sidewalk edge.

[SHOW: teacher stopping at a landmark and pointing it out]

Keep the pace comfortable. The goal here is awareness, not fitness. You want every student to see the full boundary.

### Segment 2: Inside or Outside Quiz (5 min)

[SHOW: teacher pointing to various spots while students respond]

Gather students in the center. Point to landmarks and locations and ask, "Inside or outside?" Point to the basketball hoop: inside. Point to the parking lot: outside. Point to the garden bed: inside, but off limits.

This is quick and interactive. Keep students calling out answers together.

### Segment 3: Off-Limits Areas (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher standing near a garden bed or parking lot entrance]

Walk students to any off-limits areas that fall inside the boundary. Gardens, parking lots, equipment storage, doorways. Name them clearly. "This is inside our boundary, but you still can't go here."

### Segment 4: Why Boundaries Matter (2 min)

[SHOW: teacher with students gathered in a circle]

Ask students why boundaries matter. Guide them toward two ideas: safety (so nobody runs into traffic or gets lost) and respect (for property, for other people's space). Keep this brief and conversational.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- What does boundary mean?
- How can crossing the boundary be dangerous?
- What are some things we see every day that mark boundaries? (Fences, curbs, signs)

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

If you have extra time or want to revisit this lesson:

- Repeat the boundary at a faster pace
- Travel the boundary with different movements (skipping, galloping)
- **Hot or Cold**: one student hides near a cone, and a partner uses temperature clues to find them
- **Shrinking Boundary Tag**: play tag while the boundary gets smaller each round
- **Capture the Flag**: two teams with separate territories; cross the boundary to grab the flag

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. Walk the space before class. Unclear edges confuse students, so place cones where the boundary isn't obvious.
2. Make the inside/outside quiz fast-paced and fun. Students should be calling out answers, not raising hands.
3. Be explicit about off-limits areas. Students remember what you name directly much better than vague warnings.
