# Video Script: Lesson 2 - Match the Code

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 2 adds structure to the exploring from Lesson 1: find them in this specific order. Students use clue sheets to visit animal checkpoints in sequence, and they learn the habit that defines orienteering: confirming you are at the right checkpoint by checking its code. In Animal-O, the code is an animal picture.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints: cones with animal pictures
- Clue sheets (multiple courses and difficulty levels)
- Start/finish markers
- A gym, schoolyard, or local park with an established boundary

Before class: place animal checkpoints and start/finish markers around the space.

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Use a clue sheet to complete a course in order
- Confirm you are at the right checkpoint by checking its code, as orienteers always do
- Build spatial memory by repeating courses
- Learn that orienteering is a timed activity, and try to beat your own time
- Check a partner's work by confirming they visited the correct checkpoint

### PE Standards (SHAPE America, 2024)
- Combines varied locomotor skills in a variety of practice tasks (1.5.1)
- Demonstrates respectful behaviors that contribute to positive social interaction in group activities (3.5.3)
- Solves problems independently, with partners, and in small groups (3.5.5)
- Identifies movement strengths and opportunities for practice for individual improvement (4.5.7)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. About 5 minutes for the Explore & Find warm-up, then 25 to 50 minutes for the Animal-O progression, with the Animal Relay as a closer. The progression has built-in levels, so the lesson naturally fills whatever time you have.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Explore & Find Warm-Up (5 min)

[SHOW: pairs of students spreading out and finding animal cones]

Start with a quick round of Explore & Find from Lesson 1. Students explore to find animal checkpoints and return on the gathering signal. Ask: "How many can you remember from last time?"

### Segment 2: Introduce Clue Sheets (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher holding up a clue sheet and explaining it to the group]

Pair students up. Show them a clue sheet. "This tells you which animals to find and in what order. Go to the first animal, check the picture to make sure you're at the right one, then move to the next."

[SHOW: close-up of a clue sheet strip with a start triangle, animal pictures in sequence, and a finish circle]

Emphasize: checking the animal picture at each checkpoint is how you confirm you're at the right place. If the picture doesn't match, keep looking.

### Segment 3: Animal-O Progression (20-40 min)

[SHOW: pairs of students moving between checkpoints with clue sheets in hand]

This is the core of the lesson, with a natural progression built in:

**Level 1: 5-animal clue sheet**

[SHOW: pair completing a course, checking animals at each cone]

Start everyone with a 5-animal clue sheet. This teaches the process: read the clue, find the checkpoint, check the animal, move to the next.

**Level 2: Different clue sheets**

[SHOW: pairs taking different routes across the space]

The kit includes six different 5-animal courses, so pairs can try new sequences. A new sheet forces them to plan new routes instead of following the same path.

**Level 3: From memory, with a Checker**

[SHOW: one student running the course while their partner watches with the clue sheet]

Pairs repeat a course they know, but now one partner navigates from memory while the other keeps the clue sheet as the Checker. At each checkpoint, the Navigator calls out the animal name and the Checker says "correct" or "incorrect." Then they switch roles. This introduces checking as a partner skill.

**Level 4: For speed, with self-checking**

[SHOW: student running between checkpoints with urgency, glancing at their own clue sheet]

Finally, students time themselves. "Can you beat your last time?" Now they self-check: carry the clue sheet and confirm each animal before moving on. Orienteering is a timed activity, and knowing where things are makes you faster.

### Segment 4: Animal Relay (5-8 min)

[SHOW: students in small teams taking turns running to named checkpoints]

Close with the Animal Relay. In small teams, students take turns running to a named animal checkpoint and back. The caller says an animal name; the runner finds it and returns. It tests spatial memory and adds competitive energy.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- What do you like most about orienteering so far?
- What helped you get faster?
- At each checkpoint, how do you check you are at the right place?
- How did you remember where the animals are?
- How did you work together?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- Track course completion or times on a whiteboard
- Move the checkpoints to new locations and start over
- Have students draw a map of the area showing checkpoint locations

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. Have multiple clue sheets ready at different levels. Students finish at different speeds, and you don't want anyone standing around waiting.
2. Emphasize checking the animal picture at every checkpoint. Students will want to skip this step, but confirming your location is the core orienteering habit, and it's how partners can check each other without electronic timing.
3. Let pairs move through the progression at their own pace rather than advancing the whole class together.
