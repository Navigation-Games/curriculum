# Video Script: Lesson 3 - Explore & Find

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 3 introduces checkpoints for the first time. Students explore the space in pairs to find animal checkpoints, then return on signal to report what they found and where it was. This lesson builds spatial memory and descriptive language that students will need when they start using clue sheets and maps.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints: cones with animal pictures attached
- Whistle or flag (gathering signal)
- A gym, schoolyard, or local park with an established boundary
- Place animal checkpoints around the space before class. Spread them out, and tuck a few in less obvious spots.

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Explore a space and find checkpoints within the boundary
- Return on the gathering signal
- Describe what you found and where it was, using spatial language
- Begin building spatial memory of checkpoint locations
- Work with a partner

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Demonstrate locomotor skills (S1.E1, S1.E2)
- Engage actively in class (S3.E2)
- Work cooperatively with others (S4.E5)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 45 minutes. About 3 minutes for the Boundary Run review, 20 to 30 minutes for Explore & Find rounds (including reporting back), and 5 minutes for the Animal Relay and reflection.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Boundary Run Review (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher jogging the boundary with students]

Start with a quick Boundary Run. By now students should know the boundary well. This is a brief warm-up, not a teaching moment.

### Segment 2: Pair Up and Explore (8-10 min)

[SHOW: teacher pairing students, then pairs spreading out across the space]

Pair students up. Explain the task: "There are animal checkpoints hidden around the space. With your partner, explore within the boundary to find as many as you can. When you hear the whistle, come straight back."

[SHOW: pairs of students discovering cones with animal pictures]

Let pairs explore for several minutes. They should be moving constantly, covering different parts of the space.

### Segment 3: Report Back (5 min)

[SHOW: students gathered at home base, hands raised, describing locations]

Blow the gathering signal and bring everyone back. Now ask: "Which animals did you find? Where were they?"

Encourage spatial language. "The elephant was near the fence." "The giraffe was in the far corner by the tree." Build a group picture of the space together.

[SHOW: teacher pointing toward different areas as students describe them]

This reporting step is essential. It builds the descriptive vocabulary students will use with clue sheets and maps.

### Segment 4: Second Round (5-8 min)

[SHOW: students heading out again with more purpose and direction]

Send pairs out again. "Can you find more this time? Can you remember where the ones you already saw are?"

Students will move with more purpose now. They're using the spatial memory from Round 1 plus the reports from other pairs.

### Segment 5: Animal Relay (5-8 min)

[SHOW: students in small teams taking turns running to specific checkpoints]

Finish with the Animal Relay. Students take turns running to named animal checkpoints and back. This adds speed and tests their memory of where each checkpoint is.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- How many animals did you find? Which was the hardest to find?
- Where were the animals? Can you describe where each one was?
- Did it help to know where they were from the first round?
- Were some animals easier or harder to find? Why?
- How did you work with your partner?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- Move the checkpoints to new locations and start over
- Have students draw a map of the area and checkpoint locations
- Visit every animal and move like that animal to the next one

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. Place some checkpoints in easy-to-spot locations and a few in trickier ones. A mix keeps all students engaged, whether they're quick explorers or more methodical.
2. Don't skip the reporting step. The spatial language students practice here ("near the fence," "between the trees") is the foundation for reading clue sheets and maps in later lessons.
3. Keep the gathering signal consistent. Students need to return promptly every time. If returns are slow, shorten the explore time so the signal stays meaningful.
