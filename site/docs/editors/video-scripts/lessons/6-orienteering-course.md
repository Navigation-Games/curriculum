# Video Script: Lesson 6 - Orienteering Course

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 6 is the culmination of the progression: a real orienteering course, visiting checkpoints in order from start to finish. Everything from Lessons 1 through 5 comes together: boundary awareness, spatial memory, checking codes, map orientation, and route choice. The goal is independent navigation.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Orienteering maps
- Checkpoint markers (cones or flags)
- An all-checkpoints map (for teacher reference and checkpoint placement)
- Scorecards and pencils
- A schoolyard or local park with real features for map symbols

Before class: place checkpoints according to the all-checkpoints map.

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Navigate a course visiting checkpoints in a specific order
- Make route choices between checkpoints
- Track your position on the map as you move
- Complete courses with increasing independence

### PE Standards (SHAPE America, 2024)
- Combines varied locomotor skills in a variety of practice tasks (1.5.1)
- Applies movement concepts and strategies for safe movement within dynamic environments (2.5.1)
- Demonstrates problem-solving strategies in a variety of games/activities (2.5.5)
- Demonstrates safe behaviors independently with limited reminders (3.5.4)
- Explains the rationale for one's choices related to physical activity based on personal interests (4.5.4)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. About 5 to 10 minutes for a Score-O warm-up, then 20 to 45 minutes progressing through Point-to-Point courses, from pairs to solo. This lesson works well across multiple sessions.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Score-O Warm-Up (5-10 min)

[SHOW: students spreading out with maps, visiting checkpoints in any order]

Start with a quick Score-O round to review map skills: orient the map, read features, record codes. Keep it short. This is a refresher, not the main event.

### Segment 2: Point-to-Point (15-25 min)

[SHOW: a student tracing the line on their map from the start triangle through numbered circles]

Introduce the classic orienteering course: checkpoints must be visited in order, from the start triangle to the finish double circle. "In Score-O you chose your order. Today the map decides the order, and you decide the route between checkpoints."

Start with a short course of 3-4 checkpoints, then advance to longer courses.

[SHOW: student pausing at a junction, looking between two possible paths, choosing one]

This is where route choice matters: between any two checkpoints, there is more than one way to go, and reading the map tells you which is faster or safer.

### Segment 3: Partner Orienteering (10-15 min)

[SHOW: pair planning a route on their map together, then executing it]

Pairs plan and execute a full course together, talking through route decisions at each checkpoint.

### Segment 4: Solo Orienteering (10-15 min)

[SHOW: individual student navigating with a map, checking a code at a checkpoint]

Confident students do a course on their own. Emphasize self-checking at every checkpoint: read the code and confirm it matches before moving on. This is the goal of the entire progression: independent map-based navigation.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- How did you decide which way to go between checkpoints?
- Was it harder to navigate in order than in any order (Score-O)?
- What did you do when you got lost or confused?
- How did working with a partner help? What was different about going solo?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- **Symbol Relay**: team competition to learn map symbols
- **Place Your Own Checkpoints**: students place markers using the map, then others find them
- Relay race: teams take turns completing the course
- Challenge students to complete a course from memory
- Time students on the same course and track improvement

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. Don't rush the progression to independence. Pair courses first, solo second, and it's fine to spread this lesson across two or three sessions.
2. Offer courses at more than one length so students can choose their challenge. Choosing a harder course is itself a navigation decision.
3. Make self-checking the habit: code first, then move on. Students who check every code are doing exactly what competitive orienteers do.
