# Video Script: Lesson 4 - Map Symbols

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 4 introduces the real orienteering map. Symbols tell you what features are on the ground: every color, shape, and line on the map means something you can see and touch. Students find checkpoints by matching symbols to features in Symbol-O, then walk a route together on the Map Walk.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Orienteering maps
- Symbol reference cards or legend handouts
- Checkpoint markers
- A schoolyard or local park with real features (this lesson needs more than just a gym)

Before class: choose a route through the space with varied features (paths, trees, fences, buildings, open ground) and place checkpoints at features that correspond to distinct symbols.

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Identify basic orienteering map symbols (path, building, fence, tree, open ground, water)
- Use the legend to decode unfamiliar symbols
- Match symbols on the map to features on the ground
- Walk a route while keeping the map oriented
- Track your position on the map as you move

### PE Standards (SHAPE America, 2024)
- Applies movement concepts and strategies for safe movement within dynamic environments (2.5.1)
- Demonstrates problem-solving strategies in a variety of games/activities (2.5.5)
- Uses communication skills to negotiate roles and responsibilities in a physical activity setting (3.5.2)
- Solves problems independently, with partners, and in small groups (3.5.5)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. About 3 minutes for the Boundary Run, 5 minutes for the symbol introduction, 10 to 20 minutes for Symbol-O, 15 to 20 minutes for the Map Walk, and the Symbol Relay if time allows.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Boundary Run (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher jogging the boundary with students]

Quick Boundary Run to warm up, especially if this space is new.

### Segment 2: Symbol Introduction (5 min)

[SHOW: teacher holding up an orienteering map, students gathered around]

Gather students with maps. Compare the orienteering map to the pattern maps from Lesson 3. "In Lesson 3, the map was just dots. This map is a picture of the actual space. Every symbol means something real."

[SHOW: close-up of map, teacher pointing from a symbol to the real path behind them]

Point to a nearby feature, a path, a building, a tree, and find its symbol on the map. Orient the map so the symbols line up with the real features. Introduce the legend: the decoder for symbols you don't know yet.

### Segment 3: Symbol-O (10-20 min)

[SHOW: pairs of students checking their maps, then running to a fence corner]

In pairs, students find checkpoints placed at features that match specific symbols. Start with 3-4 easy symbols: path, building, fence. Then add harder ones: vegetation boundaries, small features.

### Segment 4: Map Walk (15-20 min)

[SHOW: teacher walking a route with students, everyone holding maps, stopping to compare map to ground]

Walk a route as a group. The teacher leads; students follow along on their maps. Stop at each feature: "What symbol is this on your map? Point to where we are."

[SHOW: students rotating their maps as the group turns a corner]

Students practice keeping the map oriented as the group turns corners. When you turn, the map doesn't turn with you; you rotate it so it stays matched to the world.

### Segment 5: Symbol Relay (if time)

[SHOW: teams racing to match symbol cards]

If time allows, run the Symbol Relay: teams race to match symbols to features. It's a fun, competitive way to cement symbol knowledge.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- Which symbols were easiest to recognize? Which were hardest?
- How is the orienteering map different from the pattern maps we used before?
- What helped you keep track of where you were on the map?
- When you turned a corner, what did you have to do with your map?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- **Symbol Relay**: team competition to identify and locate map symbols
- Students create their own symbol quiz for a partner
- Walk a new route; students lead and narrate what they see on the map
- Identify features visible from one spot and find all of them on the map

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. This lesson needs real features. If your only space is a gym, use the Indoor Orienteering alternative lesson instead.
2. Keep the first symbol set small. Path, building, and fence carry most early navigation; exotic symbols can wait for the legend.
3. On the Map Walk, ask "point to where we are on the map" at every stop. If students can't, slow down; that skill is the whole point of the lesson.
