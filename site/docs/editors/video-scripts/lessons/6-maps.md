# Video Script: Lesson 6 - Maps

**Duration target:** 6-7 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 6 is the culmination of the progression. Students navigate using real orienteering maps with symbols, features, and route choices. Everything from Lessons 1 through 5 comes together here: boundary awareness, returning, spatial memory, clue sheets, and map orientation.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Orienteering maps (printed, enough for each student or pair)
- Checkpoint markers (cones or flags)
- All-checkpoints map (for teacher reference and checkpoint placement)
- A schoolyard or local park with real features for map symbols (this lesson needs more than just a gym)
- Place checkpoints according to the all-checkpoints map before class

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Understand what an orienteering map is and how it differs from a pattern map
- Learn map symbols and match them to real features
- Walk a route while reading the map
- Make route choices based on the map
- Complete courses independently

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Combine movement concepts with skills (S2.E1)
- Use simple strategies and tactics (S2.E5)
- Move confidently and safely in open spaces (S2.E1, S4.E6)
- Cooperate and learn with others (S4.E4)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes, and this lesson can extend across multiple sessions. About 3 minutes for the Boundary Run, 5 minutes for the map discussion, 5 minutes for the Map Walk, and 15 to 45 minutes for Score-O and independent orienteering.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Boundary Run (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher jogging the boundary with students in the outdoor space]

Run the boundary of the space. This is familiar territory by now, but the outdoor setting may be different from previous lessons if you're moving from the schoolyard to a park.

### Segment 2: Map Discussion (5 min)

[SHOW: teacher holding up an orienteering map, students gathered around]

Introduce the orienteering map. Compare it to the pattern maps from Lesson 5. "In Lesson 5, the map was just dots and lines. This map is a picture of the actual space. Every symbol means something real."

[SHOW: close-up of map with teacher pointing to symbols]

Orient the map using real features visible from where you're standing. Point out symbols and what they represent: green for thick vegetation, white for open forest, brown lines for terrain, the circle for a checkpoint location.

Ask students to find things on the map that they can see in front of them.

### Segment 3: Map Walk (5 min)

[SHOW: teacher walking a route with students, everyone holding maps, stopping to compare map to ground]

Walk a route as a group. At each stop, match symbols on the map to the real features around you. "What does this green area on the map look like in real life? Look around. That's the hedge."

[SHOW: students looking between their maps and the landscape]

Go slowly. This is a teaching walk, not a race. Students need time to connect the abstract symbols to concrete objects.

### Segment 4: Score-O (10-20 min)

[SHOW: students fanning out across the space with maps]

Hand out maps with checkpoint circles marked. Explain Score-O: "Visit as many checkpoints as you can in the time limit, in any order. You choose your own route."

[SHOW: a pair studying their map, pointing, then heading off in a chosen direction]

This is where route choice comes in. Students must read the map to decide where to go first and how to connect checkpoints efficiently.

Set a time limit (10 to 15 minutes is typical). Blow the whistle to call everyone back.

### Segment 5: Partner and Solo Orienteering (10-20 min)

[SHOW: pair planning a route on their map together, then executing it]

After Score-O, transition to full courses. Start with partner orienteering, where pairs plan and execute a course together.

[SHOW: individual student navigating with a map]

Then let confident students try a course solo. This is the goal of the entire progression: independent map-based navigation.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- How is the orienteering map different from the pattern maps we used before?
- Which symbols were easier or harder to remember? Why?
- What strategy did you use to visit the most checkpoints in Score-O?
- How did you determine which way to go for each checkpoint?
- What shortcuts between checkpoints did you find?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- **Symbol Relay**: team competition to learn map symbols
- **Poker-O**: each checkpoint has a playing card; collect the best poker hand
- **Poison-O**: wrong checkpoints cost points, which rewards careful map reading
- **Place Your Own Checkpoints**: students place markers using the map, then others find them
- Relay race: teams take turns completing the course
- Challenge students to complete three checkpoints from memory

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. The Map Walk is not optional. Students who skip straight to Score-O will struggle with symbols and orientation. Walk the route together first, even if students are eager to run.
2. For Score-O, start with a generous time limit. Students need time to study the map and make decisions. You can shorten the time in later sessions once they're more confident.
3. This lesson works best across multiple class sessions. One session for the Map Walk and Score-O, a second for partner courses, and a third for solo navigation. Don't rush the progression to independence.
