# Applying for a Grant for Kickstart Orienteering

Most education foundation applications ask the same handful of questions. Below are the ones we've seen, with example answers you can adapt. Change the parts in [brackets] to fit your school, and write in your own words: the committee wants to hear why *you* want to do this.

For where to find a grant, see [Funding for Kickstart Orienteering](/kickstart-funding/). We'd be glad to read a draft. Send it to admin@navigationgames.org.

## Before you start

- **Talk to your principal first.** Many foundations send each application to the principal of every school it affects, and to the district's PD director if the budget includes professional development. They should hear about it from you first.
- **Read what the foundation won't fund.** Common exclusions are substitute teachers, food, replacing old equipment, and anything bought before the grant money arrives. Orienteering is a new program, so the kit is new equipment, not a replacement.
- **Ask your business office how to pay us.** Some districts can only pay approved vendors. Ask us for a quote and anything else they need.
- **List other funding.** Many forms ask what other money is going into the project. Orienteering USA's Youth Map Program often pays about half the cost of a first map, so put that here.
- **Check the timing.** Decisions often take a month or more, and the money can take a few more weeks to reach you. Most grants must be spent within a year. Maps can take several months, so apply for your map early.
- **Expect follow-up questions.** Reviewers may ask, for example, whether other schools could share the equipment, or what it costs to keep going in later years. A quick answer helps.

## Budget

Forms usually sort costs into categories. Here's how Kickstart's parts usually fit. Leave out what you don't need.

| Item | Cost | Usual category |
|---|---|---|
| Online training, including two consultations with Navigation Games | $185 | Professional development |
| Beginning Orienteering Instruction Kit | $250 | Equipment |
| Orienteering map of the school grounds (Navigation Games' share) | $300 | Fees or Other |
| Site visit from Navigation Games (optional) | $1,000 | Fees |
| **Total without the site visit** | **$735** | |

The kit price may rise to $300 in 2027. Check [the Kickstart page](/kickstart/) for current prices.

## Example questions and answers

### Summary of your project

*A short paragraph, often shown to the whole committee.*

> I'd like to bring orienteering to [school]. In orienteering, you use a map to find a series of checkpoints. Our [number] students in grades [grades] will play navigation games in PE, learn to read a map of our own school grounds, and spend more of their class time moving outdoors. The grant pays for training for me, a teaching kit, and an orienteering map of our school, through Navigation Games' Kickstart Orienteering program.

### How will the grant funds be used?

*Often with: how did you choose these items, and who will pay to replace supplies?*

> The online training (eight short units with two consultations) prepares me to teach a unit of orienteering, using Navigation Games' lesson plans, which are written for PE classes and aligned with SHAPE America standards. The kit has everything for the first lessons, which don't need a map. The map is drawn to orienteering standards by a mapper, through Orienteering USA's Youth Map Program, which pays about half its cost. I chose this program because it gives me the training, equipment and map together, and because the lessons start in the schoolyard with no special space needed.
>
> The kit is durable and will last for years. Printed maps are the only supply we'll need to replace, and our department can print those.

### Who will be affected, and how?

> In the first year, about [number] students in grades [grades] will take part in a [number]-lesson orienteering unit in PE. They'll learn to read a map, plan a route, and find their way around a space they know, while running. Orienteering works for students of every fitness level, because thinking matters as much as speed, and students who don't shine in ball sports often do well. Once the map exists, other teachers can use it too, for example in math (scale and distance) or social studies (maps and directions).

### How is this project innovative or enriching?

> No one at [school] teaches orienteering now. It combines physical activity with spatial thinking and problem solving in a way most PE units don't, and it takes students outdoors into their own school grounds. It also connects students to a lifelong sport: [local orienteering club] holds events near us that families can attend.

### How does it connect to the curriculum or to district priorities?

> The lessons are aligned with the SHAPE America PE standards, including [standard numbers from the lesson plans you'll use]. [Add a state standard or district priority, such as social-emotional learning, outdoor learning, or physical activity for all students.] Orienteering also connects to math (scale, distance, angles), science (observing and predicting), and social studies (maps and geography).

### Who will be involved?

> I'll teach the unit in my PE classes. [Principal's name] supports the project. [Other PE teachers or classroom teachers who may use the map or equipment.] Navigation Games, a nonprofit that has run orienteering programs for schools since 2015, provides the training and two consultations, and Orienteering USA's Youth Map Program arranges the map.

### Timeline

> - [Month]: Apply for the school map. Start the online training.
> - [Month]: Finish the planning units and plan the unit with Navigation Games. The kit arrives.
> - [Month]: Teach the first lessons with the kit, which don't need a map.
> - [Month]: The map is ready. Teach the map lessons and design courses on our school grounds.
> - [Month]: Review the unit with Navigation Games and plan next year. Send the final report to the foundation.

### How will you measure success?

> - Students can orient a map and use it to find checkpoints on our school grounds by the end of the unit.
> - Student feedback after the unit: what they learned, and whether they want to do it again.
> - My reflections after each class, which are part of the training, and a short survey after the unit.
> - [Number] students take part, and [number] other teachers use the map or kit by the end of the year.

### What lasting impact will this have?

*Often asked of larger grants, and sometimes as "How will this continue after the grant?"*

> The map and kit stay at the school and can be used every year by any teacher. I'll be trained to teach the unit on my own, and the lesson plans are online for the next teacher. Orienteering can be taught in every season and at every grade, so it can grow from one unit into a regular part of our PE program.

### How will you share what you learn with colleagues?

> I'll run a short session for the [PE department / school staff] at a staff meeting or PD day, so other teachers see how the activities work. The map and kit will be available to anyone in the building, and I'll help colleagues plan their first lessons.

### Could other schools share the equipment, or do the same thing?

> Yes. Kickstart provides one kit per district, and teachers at different schools share it by scheduling who uses it when. Each school would need its own map. Another school could follow the same plan, with its own teacher taking the training.

### Are there ongoing costs?

> Very few. The kit is durable and the map lasts for years. A map may need an update after major construction on the grounds. Printing maps is the only regular cost.
