# Funding for Kickstart Orienteering

If your school is paying for [Kickstart Orienteering](/kickstart/) or part of it, here are the places teachers most often find the money. The program has separate parts (training, kit, map), so you can ask different funders for different parts.

## Your school's education foundation

Many school districts have a local education foundation: a nonprofit that raises money for the district's schools, often named for the town or county. Many of them run small grants that teachers can apply for, usually a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, for a new program or equipment. That's a good fit for a teaching kit and a school map.

How common they are depends on where you are. Some estimates say nearly half of US districts have one, though others count far fewer. In Florida, nearly every county district has one, and the state matches some of their grants. Smaller and rural districts are less likely to have one, or have a small volunteer-run version.

To find yours, search for your town or district name plus "education foundation", or ask your principal or district office. Not every foundation takes applications from teachers, so look for words like "teacher grants", "mini-grants" or "innovation grants" on its website, and check the deadline. Many have one round a year.

For the questions these applications usually ask, with example answers, see [Applying for a Grant for Kickstart Orienteering](/kickstart-grant-application/). We'd be glad to help you with the application. Write to admin@navigationgames.org.

## Your PTO or PTA

Parent groups often pay for equipment and special programs, and orienteering is something parents can see and join in. Ask early in the school year, before their budget is spent.

## Your school and district

- **Professional development funds.** The online training [counts as professional development](/pd-credit/) in many districts. Ask whether your PD budget can pay for it.
- **Your PE department budget** may cover the kit and map as equipment.
- **Title IV-A** (Student Support and Academic Enrichment) is federal money that districts can use for a well-rounded education and for safe and healthy students, which includes physical education and physical activity. Rules vary by state. Ask your district's Title IV-A coordinator whether orienteering fits their plan.

## The map: Orienteering USA's Youth Map Program

[Orienteering USA's Youth Map Program](/reference/equipment/maps/) often pays for about half the cost of a first map of a school or park. Unit 1 of the training helps you apply.

## Crowdfunding: DonorsChoose

[DonorsChoose](https://www.donorschoose.org/) lets public school teachers post a classroom project for donors to fund. It works well for equipment like the teaching kit. You need to be a full-time teacher (or nearly full-time) at a public or charter school. Look out for their matching offers, which change through the year.

## Physical activity and PE grants

- **[NFL PLAY 60 grants](https://www.heart.org/NFLPLAY60)** from the American Heart Association pay for physical activity equipment and inclusive PE, and accept applications year-round.
- **Your state's SHAPE America chapter** (often called the state AHPERD) may offer small grants to members. [SHAPE America](https://www.shapeamerica.org/) lists national grants too.

## Local businesses and community groups

Rotary, Lions and other service clubs, local businesses and your community foundation often support school projects in their town. If your map would include a park or conservation land, the landowner or a local land trust may want to help, since a map gets more people using it well.
