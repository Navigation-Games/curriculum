# 2 - Find Your Way Home

---
tagline: Navigate back to home base from an unfamiliar starting point
epigraph: Can you find your way back?
sidebar_position: 2
time: 45-60 minutes
space: Schoolyard, local park, or camp area
materials:
  - Orienteering maps or simple maps of the area (1 per pair)
  - A "home base" marker (flag or cone)
  - Checkpoints (optional, for added challenge)
setup: Identify home base and several starting locations at varying distances and complexity
vocabulary:
  - Orient the map
  - Feature
  - Home base
activities: []
---

## Goals

- Orient the map to match the real world
- Use visible features to determine your location on the map
- Navigate from an unfamiliar location back to a known destination
- Build confidence navigating independently


## Delivery

1. **Safety briefing** (5 min): review boundaries and gathering signal. Emphasize: if you are ever truly unsure where you are, stop and wait. A leader will find you.
2. **Introduce the map** (5 min): hand out maps. Orient the maps together at home base. Point out home base on the map. Identify visible features and find them on the map.
3. **Guided walk-out** (10 min): walk the group to a nearby location. Stop and ask: "Where are we on the map? Which way is home base?" Have them orient the map and point toward home base. Walk back together.
4. **Find Your Way Home** (20-30 min): a leader walks each pair to a starting location. The pair uses the map to navigate back to home base on their own. Start with easy locations (short distance, on a path) and progress to harder ones (farther away, off-trail, or with turns).
   - When a pair arrives at home base, walk them to a new starting point
   - Increase difficulty each round
5. **Wrap-up** (5 min): gather at home base. What features helped you find your way back? Was any starting point especially tricky?


## Compact Delivery

1. **Safety**: boundaries, gathering signal, stop-and-wait rule
2. **Introduce the map**: orient at home base, identify features
3. **Guided walk-out**: walk to a location, orient map, point to home base, walk back
4. **Find Your Way Home**: pairs are walked to starting points, navigate back independently; increase difficulty each round
5. **Wrap-up**


## Reflection

- What features on the map helped you find your way back?
- How did you know which direction to go?
- Was it harder from farther away? What made it harder?
- How did you orient the map?


## Extensions

- Add checkpoints along the route that pairs must visit on their way home
- Time each return trip and see if pairs can improve
- Have campers write directions for someone else to follow
