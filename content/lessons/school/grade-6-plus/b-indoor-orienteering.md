## Goals

### Orienteering Goals
- Build a world and represent it accurately on a map, or build a world to match a given map
- Apply route choice to plan efficient courses around obstacles
- Design and record a course precise enough for a peer to run and self-check
- Compare the two directions, world-to-map and map-to-world, and explain what each teaches about the map-terrain relationship

### PE Standards (SHAPE America, 2024)

**Standard 2: Movement & Fitness Concepts**
- Identifies the effective use of movement concepts within multiple dynamic environments (2.8.1). *Students plan routes through a built world, applying route choice to work around obstacles.*
- Demonstrates problem-solving skills in a variety of games and activities (2.8.7). *Students represent a built world accurately on a map, or build a world accurately from a map.*

**Standard 3: Social Skills**
- Uses communication skills to negotiate strategies and tactics in a physical activity setting (3.8.3). *Groups decide on a layout and rules together, then negotiate how to represent it on a map.*
- Solves problems with teammates and opponents (3.8.8). *Students check a peer's recorded course against what actually happened when running it.*

**Standard 4: Personal Skills**
- Choice and interests impact engagement (4.8.4). *Students choose whether to build first and map second, or map first and build second.*
- Reflects on movement experiences (4.8.9). *Students compare what building-then-mapping and mapping-then-building each taught them.*


## Delivery

1. [**Build the World**](/activities/core/build-the-world): in groups, build a world using available equipment. Decide the rules and be ready to explain them
2. Explore each other's worlds. Discuss route choice: "If you had to get from here to there, what's your best path around this obstacle?"
3. Draw a map of your own world, including obstacles and checkpoints
4. Design and record a course on your map. Trade with another group
5. Run the course you received, using route choice to plan your path; record your own checkpoint sequence as you go
6. Compare your recorded sequence to the map's designer. Discuss any mismatches
7. [**Match the World to the Map**](/activities/core/build-the-world): reverse it. One group hands another a finished map, without showing the built world; that group builds a world to match it, then the two groups compare
8. Debrief: which direction, world-to-map or map-to-world, was harder? Why?


## Compact Delivery

1. **Build the World**: groups build a world and agree on rules
2. Discuss route choice around obstacles
3. Draw a map of your world
4. Design and record a course; trade with another group
5. Run the course using route choice; record your sequence
6. Compare recorded sequences with the designer
7. **Match the World to the Map**: reverse direction, build from a map instead
8. Debrief: which direction was harder?


## Reflection

- What route choice did you make around a tricky obstacle? Why?
- How did you decide what belonged on the map and what could be left off?
- When you built a world from someone else's map, what was hard to interpret?
- Which direction, building-to-map or map-to-building, revealed more mistakes? Why do you think that is?
- How did your group divide the work of building, mapping, and recording?


## Extensions

- Time both the course-running and the world-building-from-map steps; compare
- Introduce compass bearings for the map-first variation: describe obstacle placement using cardinal directions
- Have one group build a world with a "trick" obstacle, something that looks passable but isn't, and see if the mapping group catches it
