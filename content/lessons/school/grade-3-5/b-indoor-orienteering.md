## Goals

### Orienteering Goals
- Use available equipment to build a world with clear obstacles and rules
- Draw a map of a world you built, including its obstacles and checkpoints
- Design a course and record its checkpoint sequence so a partner can check it
- Run a course someone else designed, using their recorded checkpoint sequence to self-check
- Understand that a map can represent a place you already know, or guide you to build a place you don't

### PE Standards (SHAPE America, 2024)

**Standard 2: Movement & Fitness Concepts**
- Applies movement concepts and strategies for safe movement within dynamic environments (2.5.1). *Students navigate around obstacles they helped build, applying the world's rules to move safely.*
- Demonstrates problem-solving strategies in a variety of games/activities (2.5.5). *Students plan a course through their built world and figure out how to represent its obstacles on a map.*

**Standard 3: Social Skills**
- Negotiates roles and responsibilities in a physical activity setting (3.5.2). *Groups decide together which obstacles to build, where, and what the rules are.*
- Solves problems independently, with partners, and in small groups (3.5.5). *Groups check a partner group's recorded course and confirm the sequence is correct.*

**Standard 4: Personal Skills**
- Explains rationale for choices based on personal interests (4.5.4). *Students explain why they chose a particular obstacle or route on their map.*
- Identifies movement strengths and opportunities for practice for individual improvement (4.5.7). *Students repeat courses they designed, refining their map as they find mistakes.*


## Delivery

1. [**Boundary Run**](/activities/core/boundary-run): quick review of the gym boundary
2. [**Build the World**](/activities/core/build-the-world): in small groups, use mats, ropes, tables, and chairs to build obstacles. Decide together what's passable and what's off-limits
3. Walk through each group's world as a class; each group explains its rules
4. Play an Animal-O style course through the world using a clue sheet
5. Draw a map of the world, including obstacles and checkpoints. Compare maps within your group
6. Draw a new course on the map and record its checkpoint sequence
7. Trade maps and recorded courses with another group. Run their course, then check your recorded sequence against theirs
8. **Variation:** if time allows, try [Match the World to the Map](/activities/core/build-the-world): bring a finished map and have a group build the world to match it, instead of mapping a world they built


## Compact Delivery

1. **Boundary Run**: quick review
2. **Build the World**: groups build obstacles with available equipment; agree on rules
3. Walk through each group's world as a class
4. Animal-O style course through the world
5. Draw a map of the world
6. Draw and record a new course
7. Trade with another group; run and check their course
8. Variation: build the world from a finished map instead


## Reflection

- How did you decide what an obstacle would represent: a wall, a river, a building?
- What was hard about drawing a map of a place you built yourself?
- When you recorded your course, how did you make sure someone else could check it?
- Did the group you traded with run your course correctly? What would you change about your map?
- If you built a world from a map instead, how did that change what you paid attention to?


## Extensions

- Time the recorded courses and try to beat your own time
- Combine two groups' worlds into one larger world and make a combined map
- Add a checkpoint that is only reachable one way, so route choice matters
