## Description

Line-O develops precision in map reading. A line is drawn on the map, and participants follow that line through the terrain. Along the line, unmarked checkpoints are placed at features. Participants must stay on the line, notice the checkpoints as they pass them, and remember or mark their locations. The challenge is to follow the line exactly and to recognize the terrain features where checkpoints are placed.


## Goals

- Short: Precision map reading
  Long: Develop precision in map reading

- Short: Linear features
  Long: Practise following linear features and fine navigation

- Short: Terrain to map
  Long: Improve ability to relate terrain to the map

- Short: Describe locations
  Long: Learn to describe checkpoint locations accurately


## Vocabulary

- Precision
  Navigating exactly along the intended route rather than approximately.

- Feature
  A real-world object that appears on the map. The checkpoints in Line-O are placed at features along the line.


## Setup

1. Put out checkpoints along the line route as per the all-checkpoints map


## Steps

**1. Pair up.** Have students pair up and look at the map together.

**2. Find the start.** Find where the drawn line starts on the map and in the terrain.

**3. Follow the line.** Keep the map oriented as you move. Try to stay exactly on the line. Watch for terrain features that match the map and talk about where you are.

**4. Find the checkpoints.** Look for unmarked checkpoints placed directly on the line. Remember the location of each checkpoint.

**5. Mark the map.** Draw the checkpoint locations on your map once you have finished.


## Delivery

1. Pair up and study the map; find where the line starts
2. Follow the line, keeping the map oriented
3. Watch for unmarked checkpoints at features along the line
4. Mark checkpoint locations on the map after finishing


## Reflection

- How did you make sure you stayed on the line?
- Were there parts where you went off the line? How did you know?
- How would you describe where each checkpoint was located, using the terrain around it?
- What helped you navigate most accurately?


## Adaptations

*Ways to adapt the activity to meet the needs of your students: slow things down, increase the challenge, or adapt for different learners*

- **Timed**: add timing for a competitive version
- **Fewer obvious features**: make the course harder with fewer obvious features
- **Checkpoint count**: use more or fewer checkpoint locations
- **Solo**: do it individually instead of in pairs
