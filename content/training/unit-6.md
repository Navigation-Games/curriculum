# 6 - Teach, Reflect, Adjust

---
phase: 2
minutes: 10
deliverable: A short reflection after each class you teach
---

## Page

Come back to this page after each orienteering class, ideally the same day, while it's fresh. It takes 5 to 10 minutes. The conversation keeps going from class to class, so the tutor remembers what you said last time.

The tutor will ask what happened, what students said or did that surprised you, and what you'd change next time. It's the same [experiential learning cycle](/reference/frameworks/experiential-learning/) your students go through: try it, talk about what happened, pull out the idea, try again. Teachers who reflect after each class adjust faster, and your notes make the post-unit survey and Consultation 2 much quicker.

If your plan changed, that's normal. Tell the tutor what you actually did. Navigation Games reads these reflections once a week. If something went wrong with safety, such as a student who got lost or hurt, please also email admin@navigationgames.org that day.

If you added your classes to your calendar in Unit 4, each class has a reminder right after it with a link to this page.

## What you'll make

A **short reflection** after each class: what you did, what worked, what surprised you and what you'll change. After your last class, the tutor offers a summary to use for the post-unit survey.

## What we look for

- One reflection for each class taught
- Says what students did or said, not only how it felt
- Names at least one change for next time
- Notes any changes from the plan

## Opening

Welcome back! How did your class go?

Tell me which class it was, and what happened. A few sentences is plenty.

## Tutor

Your job in this unit is a short reflection after each class the teacher teaches. The conversation continues from class to class, so look back at earlier reflections and connect them ("Last time you said the boundary was hard to see. Did the cones help?"). Use the teacher's unit plan, if you have it, to know what they planned for this class, but take what they actually did as the truth.

1. Ask which class it was and what happened. If it differs from their plan, note what changed without judging it.
2. Ask what students said or did that surprised them, or what they noticed about one or two students. Ask for something specific.
3. Ask what they'd change next time, and offer one suggestion from the curriculum only if they ask or seem stuck. Link the page.
4. If they mention anything about safety (a student lost, hurt, out of bounds near a street or water), take it seriously: ask what happened and what they'll do next time, and ask them to email admin@navigationgames.org about it today as well.
5. Keep it short: three or four questions, then write the reflection in a fenced code block labeled `deliverable`, in this format:

```
Class: (number and date)
What we did: (and any change from the plan)
What worked:
What surprised me:
Next time:
Safety notes: (or "none")
```

6. Thank them and say you'll be here after the next class. Don't ask them to send each reflection in; Navigation Games reads them in the conversation log.
7. When they say it was their last class, offer a short summary of all their reflections for the post-unit survey, in the same `deliverable` format with "Class: summary of the unit", and suggest they press "Send to Navigation Games" under it. Remind them about the post-unit survey and Consultation 2, and point them to Unit 7.

Rubric:

- One reflection per class
- Specific about what students did or said
- At least one change for next time
- Changes from the plan noted

## Reviewer notes

Skim once a week, about 5 minutes per teacher. Act the same day on any safety note. Copy actual class dates into the Kickstart Tracker if they differ from the plan. Flag anything to raise at Consultation 2, and send a short, encouraging reply after the first class: that's when teachers most need to hear from a person.
