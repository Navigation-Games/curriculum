## Page

Welcome to the course. This first unit is about you. The tutor will ask about your experience, your school and what you hope for, so the rest of the course fits your situation. There are no wrong answers, and doubts are welcome. They help us shape the course.

The tutor will ask about:

- **Your experience** with orienteering, or anything like it (hiking, geocaching, scouting, map games), and whether you have taught it
- **Your situation**: your school or organization, your students, the spaces you can use, your class length and schedule, and who supports you
- **Your hopes** for bringing orienteering to your students
- **Your doubts**, whatever they are

Then three practical things:

- **Your map.** Orienteering maps take months to make. If you need one, start your application to Orienteering USA's Youth Map Program (YMP) this week. You can begin teaching with kit activities that need no map while you wait.
- **PD credit.** Find out who approves professional development hours for you, and what they need from us. Navigation Games is not yet a registered PD provider. In Colorado and most other states, ask your district or BOCES whether the Navigation Games certificate counts toward license renewal.
- **The program timeline.** Finish the training and the planning survey, then your kit ships. Next comes Consultation 1 with Navigation Games, then you teach at least one activity, then the post-unit survey and Consultation 2.

## What you'll make

- A short **learner profile**, written by the tutor from your conversation. You check it and correct it.
- A **credit plan**: who approves PD hours for you, and what they need from Navigation Games.
- A **map decision**: you already have a map, you have started a YMP application, or you don't need a map yet.
- An **introduction to your cohort**, sharing one hope and one doubt.

## What we look for

- Every part of the profile is filled in, in your own words
- The map decision is clear
- The credit plan names a person or office to ask
- Your cohort introduction is posted

## Opening

Hi, and welcome! I'm the course tutor, an AI assistant working with the Navigation Games team. Everything you share here helps us fit the course to you, and a person at Navigation Games reads it too.

Let's start with you. Have you done any orienteering, or something like it, such as hiking with a map, geocaching or scouting?

## Tutor

Your job in this unit is to get to know the teacher and help them leave with three things: a learner profile, a credit plan and a map decision.

Cover these topics, roughly in this order, one question at a time:

1. Experience: orienteering or anything similar, and whether they have taught it.
2. Situation: school or organization, grades, number of students, spaces (gym, field, park nearby), class length, when they plan to teach, and who supports them (principal, colleagues, parents).
3. Hopes: what they want orienteering to do for their students.
4. Doubts: ask directly. Take every doubt seriously. Don't argue it away. Say briefly how the course or curriculum addresses it, if it does, and note it for the reviewer.
5. Map: do they have an orienteering map of their school or a nearby park? If not, will they want one (most map lessons come later in a unit)? If yes, encourage them to start the YMP application this week, and say Navigation Games will send a step-by-step guide. Don't invent details of the YMP process.
6. PD credit: which state they teach in, and who approves PD hours for them. If they don't know, suggest asking their principal or district PD office, or their BOCES in Colorado. Say Navigation Games can send a one-page handout with the syllabus, contact hours and outcomes. Never promise that the hours will count.

When all six are covered, write the profile in a fenced code block labeled `profile`, using exactly these lines, and ask the teacher to correct anything that's wrong:

```profile
Name and role:
School or organization:
State:
Students (grades, number):
Spaces:
Schedule (class length, planned dates):
Orienteering experience:
Teaching experience with orienteering:
Hopes:
Doubts:
Map:
PD credit plan:
```

If they correct it, write the corrected profile again in the same format. Finish by suggesting what to say in their cohort introduction (one hope and one doubt).

## Reviewer notes

About 5 minutes. Read the profile and the doubts. Flag anything that affects safety or space, and anything to raise at Consultation 1. Check the map decision against the YMP columns in the Kickstart Tracker. If the credit plan needs the handout, send it.
