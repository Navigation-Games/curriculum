# Video Script: Gathering

**Duration target:** 4-5 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers and program leaders

## Opening (visual: title card)

Gathering teaches students to come back when you call them. They move freely in the space, then return to you on a signal as fast as they can. It builds the habit of staying aware while moving, which is essential for every orienteering activity that follows.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- A defined boundary (do the Boundary Run first if students have not done it)
- A gathering signal: whistle, raised hand, flag, or verbal call
- A central gathering location

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen as bullet list)

- Students maintain awareness of a signal while moving
- Students are aware of others in shared space
- Students respond quickly and safely to the gathering signal
- Students communicate and coordinate with the group to improve gathering time
- Students move freely within a defined space with confidence

## Setup (visual: aerial/wide shot of the space being set up)

1. Make sure the boundary is clear. If students have already done the Boundary Run, a quick verbal reminder is enough.
[SHOW: teacher gesturing at the boundary edges]

2. Pick a central gathering location. Stand there. This is where students will return every time.
[SHOW: teacher standing at center, camera pulls back to show the full space]

3. Check the space for hazards. Remove anything students might trip on while running back.

## How to Run It (visual: demonstration with students)

1. Gather the group at the central location. Review the boundary briefly.
[SHOW: students standing around the teacher at center]

2. Demonstrate your gathering signal clearly. A whistle blast, a raised hand, a flag wave. Use both an audible and a visual signal so all students can participate.
[SHOW: teacher blowing whistle and raising hand simultaneously]

3. Explain the activity: "You will move freely within the boundary. When you hear this signal, return here as quickly and safely as you can."
[SHOW: teacher speaking to the group]

4. Send them out. Students walk, jog, or skip freely within the boundary. They can go individually, in pairs, or small groups.
[SHOW: wide shot of students spreading out across the space, moving in different directions]

5. After 2-3 minutes, give the signal. Start counting seconds out loud as students run back.
[SHOW: teacher blows whistle, students turn and run toward center. Teacher counting: "...eight, nine, ten, eleven..."]

6. Announce the time: "That took us 14 seconds." Challenge them to beat it.
[SHOW: teacher addressing the reassembled group]

7. Ask: "What strategies could help the whole group gather more quickly?" Let them discuss briefly.
[SHOW: students talking to each other, offering ideas]

8. Send them out again. Repeat 3-4 times total.
[SHOW: students spreading out again, then gathering faster on the next signal]

## Progression (visual: side-by-side comparison of basic vs advanced)

- **Basic**: students explore and return on signal. Count the time.
[SHOW: first round, students returning at a relaxed pace]

- **Timed challenge**: beat your previous time. The group competes against itself, not each other.
[SHOW: teacher announcing "11 seconds! Two seconds faster!"]

- **Strategy round**: give students 30 seconds to plan. "How can you get the whole group back faster?" They might decide to stay closer, spread the signal, or assign roles.
[SHOW: students huddling, then spreading out and gathering faster]

- **Different signals**: switch to visual-only signals (raised flag, raised hand) to increase difficulty. Students must keep watching, not just listening.
[SHOW: teacher raising a flag silently, students noticing and running back]

- **Movement constraints**: students must skip, gallop, or use animal movements while exploring.
[SHOW: students galloping around the space]

- **With a game**: play tag between gathering signals. When the signal goes, everyone stops tag and returns.
[SHOW: students playing tag, then freezing and running to center on the whistle]

## Common Mistakes (visual: split screen correct vs incorrect)

1. **Using only an audible signal.** A whistle alone excludes students who are hard of hearing or far away in a noisy space. Always pair an audible signal with a visual one.
[SHOW: left side, teacher only blowing whistle, distant student not noticing. Right side, teacher blowing whistle and raising flag, all students responding]

2. **Not counting the time.** Without a number to beat, there is no motivation to improve. The timed element turns a simple recall into a group challenge.
[SHOW: left side, students casually strolling back. Right side, teacher counting aloud, students sprinting]

3. **Waiting too long between signals.** If you wait 5-6 minutes, students lose awareness and get absorbed in play. Keep it to 2-3 minutes between signals, especially early on.
[SHOW: left side, students completely disengaged. Right side, students staying alert while moving]

## Wrap-up (visual: return to presenter)

Gathering builds the single most important habit for outdoor orienteering: staying aware and coming back when called. Use the gathering signal throughout every lesson, not just during this activity. It becomes your management tool for the rest of the year.
