# Video Script: Geometric-O

**Duration target:** 6-7 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers and program leaders

## Opening (visual: title card)

Geometric-O is where students use a real map for the first time. They orient a simple pattern map using colored landmarks, then visit checkpoints in order. This is the bridge between clue sheets and orienteering maps, and the whiteboard introduction is the most important part.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints: cones with animal pictures and numbers (31-40)
- 4 colored cones for the corners: red, blue, green, yellow
- Geometric-O maps: 5-checkpoint and 10-checkpoint versions
- A whiteboard and markers
- A all-checkpoints map showing the arrangement

[SHOW: close-up of numbered animal cones, colored corner cones, printed maps, and whiteboard with the pattern drawn on it]

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen as bullet list)

- Students read a simple map and connect it to the physical space
- Students use landmarks (colored cones) to orient a map
- Students describe checkpoint locations using spatial relationship vocabulary (closest, furthest, between, next to)
- Students identify and fix orientation mistakes
- Students complete progressively harder courses using a map

## Setup (visual: aerial/wide shot of the space being set up)

1. Arrange checkpoints (animal-picture cones numbered 31-40) to match the all-checkpoints map. Exact distances do not need to be perfect, but positions relative to the colored landmarks must be correct.
[SHOW: teacher placing numbered cones according to the all-checkpoints map]

2. Place colored cones in the four corners: red, blue, green, yellow.
[SHOW: teacher placing colored cones at corners, camera pulls back to show full layout]

3. Draw the arrangement on a whiteboard. Use orange dots for animal cones and colored dots for corner cones. Do not label which animal is at each location.
[SHOW: close-up of whiteboard with dots drawn in the correct pattern]

4. Set out printed maps where students can access them, ideally near a self-serve station.
[SHOW: maps fanned out on a table near the start area]

## How to Run It (visual: demonstration with students)

**Part 1: Whiteboard Introduction (do not skip this)**

1. Have students gather facing the cone arrangement. Hold up the whiteboard map upside down.
[SHOW: teacher holding whiteboard facing students, map clearly rotated wrong]

2. Ask: "What do you think the orange dots are?" Students answer: the animal cones. "What about the colored dots?" The colored corner cones.
[SHOW: students pointing and answering]

3. Point to where the red cone should be according to the upside-down map. Ask: "Is the red cone there?" Students see it is not.
[SHOW: teacher pointing to the wrong corner, students shaking their heads]

4. Ask: "Something is wrong with this map. What is it?" Students figure out it needs to be turned.
[SHOW: students calling out that it is upside down]

5. Say: "I am going to turn the map. Shout 'Stop!' when the colored dots match real life." Turn the map slowly. If students say "Stop" too early, check one corner and keep turning.
[SHOW: teacher rotating the whiteboard, students watching, then shouting "Stop!" when it lines up]

6. Now the map is oriented. Point to a checkpoint on the map and ask students to point to that cone in real life. Do this 2-3 times, using spatial vocabulary: "It is the checkpoint closest to the red cone."
[SHOW: teacher pointing at the whiteboard, students pointing at cones in the field]

**Part 2: Spatial Descriptions**

7. Ask students to find checkpoints based on descriptions of increasing difficulty:
   - "Find the animal closest to the red cone"
   - "Find the animal furthest from the blue cone"
   - "Find all the animals between the yellow and green cones"
[SHOW: students running to the described locations]

**Part 3: Courses**

8. Hand out 5-checkpoint maps. Students work in pairs, sharing one map. They visit checkpoints in order, starting with the triangle symbol and ending with the double circle.
[SHOW: pair of students studying a map, then moving to the first checkpoint]

9. After completing a course, students can repeat it for speed, try it from memory, or move to a 10-checkpoint map.
[SHOW: student returning to the start, picking up a harder map]

## Progression (visual: side-by-side comparison of basic vs advanced)

- **Younger students**: use all difficulty levels. Start with 1 checkpoint, then 2 on corners, then 2 with one off-corner, building up gradually.
[SHOW: simple 1-checkpoint map next to a 2-checkpoint map]

- **Older students**: skip to 5-checkpoint maps, then move to 10-checkpoint maps.
[SHOW: student confidently navigating a 10-checkpoint course]

- **Repeat for speed**: same course, beat your time. Builds spatial memory.
[SHOW: student running a course quickly and confidently]

- **From memory**: put the map away and complete the course.
[SHOW: student handing map to teacher, then navigating without it]

- **Remove landmarks**: take away the colored corner cones entirely. Students must orient using the checkpoint pattern alone.
[SHOW: teacher removing colored cones while students watch, then students navigating without them]

## Common Mistakes (visual: split screen correct vs incorrect)

1. **Skipping the whiteboard introduction.** This is the single biggest mistake. Without the whiteboard demo, students do not understand what "orient the map" means. They will hold the map facing themselves and get every checkpoint wrong. The whiteboard is where orientation clicks.
[SHOW: left side, student holding map facing themselves, confused. Right side, teacher doing the whiteboard demo, students engaged]

2. **Not orienting the map before each course.** Students pick up a new map and start running without turning it to match the space. Remind them: match the colored corners first, then navigate.
[SHOW: left side, student running with unoriented map. Right side, student stopping to rotate map until colors match]

3. **Placing colored cones in the wrong corners.** If the red cone is in the northeast but the map shows it in the northwest, every course will be wrong. Double-check the corner placement against the all-checkpoints map before students arrive.
[SHOW: left side, colored cones not matching the all-checkpoints map. Right side, teacher verifying each corner]

## Wrap-up (visual: return to presenter)

Geometric-O is where students go from following clue sheets to reading maps. The whiteboard introduction is non-negotiable. Once students can orient the map by matching colored landmarks, they have the foundation for every orienteering map they will ever use. Next step: a real orienteering map with the Map Walk.
