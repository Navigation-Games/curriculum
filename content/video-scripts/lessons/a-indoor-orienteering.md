# Video Script: Lesson A - Indoor Orienteering

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

This is an alternative lesson that can be used on rainy days or in schools without outdoor space. Students navigate orienteering courses on a basketball court, using the court's line markings as landmarks to orient their maps. It covers the same skills as the outdoor lessons: map orientation, route planning, and independent navigation.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints (cones or small markers)
- Basketball court maps (printed, both half-court and full-court versions)
- A gym with a basketball court
- Place checkpoints on the court according to the master map before class

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Orient a map using the court's line markings as landmarks
- Use the map to plan a route between checkpoints
- Navigate in a confined, structured space

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Use simple strategies and tactics (S2.E5)
- Move confidently and safely in open spaces (S2.E1, S4.E6)
- Work cooperatively and accept feedback from others (S4.E4, S4.E3)
- Give feedback to peers (S4.E4)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. About 3 minutes for the Boundary Run, 5 minutes for Find the Cone, 20 to 40 minutes for Basketball-O courses, and 5 minutes for reflection.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Boundary Run (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher jogging the perimeter of the gym with students]

Run the boundary of the court. In the gym, this means the walls or the outermost court lines, depending on your setup. Keep it quick.

### Segment 2: Find the Cone (5 min)

[SHOW: pairs of students holding maps and walking to specific cones on the court]

Pair students up and hand out maps. Each map shows one cone highlighted. The task: orient your map using the court lines, then go stand next to the correct cone.

[SHOW: student rotating a map to match the court orientation, then pointing and walking to a cone]

This is a simple warm-up exercise that teaches map orientation in a structured environment. The court lines (free-throw line, three-point arc, center circle, sidelines) are reliable landmarks that appear on every map.

Check each pair. If they're at the wrong cone, ask them to look at the court lines on their map and compare to the lines on the floor. Let them self-correct.

### Segment 3: Basketball-O (20-40 min)

[SHOW: pairs receiving course maps and heading out to visit checkpoints in order]

Now hand out Basketball-O course maps. Each map shows numbered checkpoints connected in order. Pairs agree on a route, then visit each checkpoint in sequence. At each one, they compare the code on the checkpoint to their answer sheet to confirm they're at the right place.

[SHOW: pair checking a code at a checkpoint, comparing to their sheet]

The progression:

**Paired courses**

[SHOW: pair navigating together, one holding the map, the other checking checkpoints]

Start with pairs. One reads the map, the other confirms checkpoints. Partners should switch roles between courses.

**Individual runs for speed**

[SHOW: individual student running a course quickly]

Once students have done a course in pairs, let them run it individually and time themselves. Repeat to build speed.

**Reverse order**

[SHOW: student heading to checkpoints in the opposite direction]

Challenge students to complete the course in reverse order. This forces them to re-read the map instead of following the memorized route.

**Without a map**

[SHOW: student navigating the course confidently without looking at a map]

For the strongest students, challenge them to complete a course from memory. This tests the spatial understanding they've built.

### Segment 4: Peer Teaching (5 min)

[SHOW: one student helping another student orient their map]

Encourage students who finish quickly to help others. They can give hints, teach map orientation, and explain how to read the court lines. This peer teaching reinforces their own understanding and builds community.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- How could you tell the difference between checkpoints?
- What shortcuts between checkpoints did you find?
- If you were at a wrong checkpoint, how did you figure out where you needed to go?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- Students may only travel along the lines of the basketball court
- Add obstacles: students may not pass any place marked with a red "X" on their map
- **Pacman-O**: play line tag while completing courses. Students must stay on the court lines while navigating to their checkpoints.

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. The basketball court is ideal for teaching map orientation because the lines are fixed, symmetrical, and visible on both the map and the floor. Point this connection out explicitly at the start.
2. Have students walk their first course before running it. Walking lets them focus on reading the map without the pressure of speed.
3. Encourage peer teaching. Students who help others orient their maps solidify their own understanding, and it keeps fast finishers engaged instead of standing idle.
