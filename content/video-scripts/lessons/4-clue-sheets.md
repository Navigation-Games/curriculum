# Video Script: Lesson 4 - Clue Sheets

**Duration target:** 5-6 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 4 adds structure to the checkpoint finding from Lesson 3. Students now use clue sheets to visit animal checkpoints in a specific order. This introduces the concept of a course and builds spatial memory through repetition. It is the bridge between free exploration and map-based navigation.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints: cones with animal pictures
- Clue sheets (printed, multiple difficulty levels)
- Start/finish markers
- A gym, schoolyard, or local park with an established boundary
- Place animal checkpoints and start/finish markers around the space before class

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Use a clue sheet to complete a course in order
- Check the animal picture at each checkpoint to confirm the right one
- Build spatial memory by repeating courses
- Complete the activity faster by remembering checkpoint locations

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Demonstrate locomotor skills (S1.E1, S1.E2)
- Engage actively in class (S3.E2)
- Accept corrective feedback from the teacher (S4.E3)
- Work cooperatively with others (S4.E5)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. About 3 minutes for the Boundary Run warm-up, then 25 to 50 minutes for the Animal-O progression. The progression has built-in levels, so the lesson naturally fills whatever time you have.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Boundary Run (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher jogging the boundary with students]

Quick Boundary Run to warm up and review the space.

### Segment 2: Pair Up and Introduce Clue Sheets (3 min)

[SHOW: teacher holding up a clue sheet and explaining it to the group]

Pair students up. Show them a clue sheet. "This tells you which animals to find and in what order. Go to the first animal, check the picture to make sure you're at the right one, then move to the next."

[SHOW: close-up of a clue sheet with 2-3 animal names/pictures in sequence]

Emphasize: checking the animal picture at each checkpoint is how you confirm you're at the right place. If the picture doesn't match, you need to keep looking.

### Segment 3: Animal-O Progression (25-50 min)

[SHOW: pairs of students moving between checkpoints with clue sheets in hand]

This is the core of the lesson, and it has a natural progression built in:

**Level 1: 5-animal clue sheet**

[SHOW: pair completing a course, checking animals at each cone]

Start everyone with a 5-animal clue sheet. This builds confidence and teaches the process: read the clue, find the checkpoint, check the animal, move to the next. There are six different 5-animal courses in the kit, so pairs can try multiple sequences.

**Level 2: 10-animal clue sheet**

[SHOW: pair receiving a 10-animal clue sheet]

Pairs who finish all six 5-animal courses get a 10-animal clue sheet. This covers more of the space and requires sustained focus.

**Level 3: Different clue sheets**

[SHOW: pairs taking different routes across the space]

Give pairs a clue sheet with the same checkpoints in a different order. This forces them to plan new routes instead of following the same path.

**Level 4: From memory**

[SHOW: pair setting down their clue sheet and heading out]

Challenge pairs to complete a course they've already done, but without looking at the clue sheet. This tests the spatial memory they've been building.

**Level 5: For speed**

[SHOW: pair running between checkpoints with urgency]

Finally, let pairs time themselves. "Can you beat your last time?" This adds motivation and shows students that knowing where things are makes you faster.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- What do you like most about orienteering so far?
- What helped you get faster?
- At each checkpoint, how do you check you are at the right place?
- How did you remember where the animals are?
- Were some courses easier or harder? Why?
- How did you work together?

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- Track course completion or times on a whiteboard
- Move the checkpoints to new locations and start over
- Have students draw a map of the area showing checkpoint locations

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. Have multiple clue sheets ready at different difficulty levels. Students finish at different speeds, and you don't want anyone standing around waiting.
2. Emphasize checking the animal picture at every checkpoint. Students will want to skip this step, but confirming location is a core orienteering skill.
3. The progression from short courses to memory-based runs happens naturally. Let students move through it at their own pace rather than advancing the whole class together.
