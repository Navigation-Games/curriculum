# Video Script: Lesson 5 - Map Readiness

**Duration target:** 6-7 minutes
**Audience:** PE teachers

## Opening (visual: title card)

Lesson 5 is where students start working with maps. They learn spatial vocabulary, practice orienting a map to match the real space, and progress through geometric pattern courses. This lesson is the bridge between clue sheets and real orienteering maps in Lesson 6.

## What You'll Need (visual: materials laid out)

- Checkpoints with animals and numbers
- 4 colored landmark cones (e.g., red, yellow, green, blue)
- Pattern maps (printed, multiple difficulty levels)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Master map (for teacher reference)
- A gym, schoolyard, or local park
- Arrange checkpoints in a geometric pattern with colored landmark cones at the corners before class

## Learning Goals (visual: goals on screen)

### Orienteering Goals
- Understand spatial relationship vocabulary (next to, closest, furthest, between)
- Orient a map using landmarks
- Read a simple map and connect it to the physical space
- Identify and fix orientation mistakes
- Complete progressively harder courses using a map

### PE Standards (SHAPE America)
- Combine movement concepts with skills (S2.E1)
- Cooperate and learn with others (S4.E4)
- Accept corrective feedback (S4.E3)

## Lesson Flow (visual: timeline graphic showing segments)

Plan for 30 to 60 minutes. About 5 minutes for the Animal-O warm-up, 10 minutes for Geometric Readiness, 15 to 35 minutes for Geometric-O courses, and 5 minutes for the Symbol-O preview and reflection.

## Delivery (visual: demonstration with students)

### Segment 1: Animal-O Warm-Up (5 min)

[SHOW: students doing a quick Animal-O course in the geometric layout]

Start with a quick Animal-O course using the geometric layout. This warms students up and gets them familiar with the checkpoint positions before you introduce the map.

### Segment 2: Geometric Readiness (10 min)

[SHOW: teacher at whiteboard with a simple dot-pattern map, students gathered facing the checkpoint layout]

Gather students so they're facing the checkpoint arrangement. Hold up the whiteboard map, but show it upside down.

[SHOW: close-up of whiteboard with dots that don't match the layout, teacher rotating it]

Ask: "What do these dots represent?" Students should recognize they're the checkpoints. Then ask: "What's wrong?" The map doesn't match the space. Slowly rotate the whiteboard while students watch.

[SHOW: students calling out "Stop!" as the map aligns with the space]

Students shout "Stop!" when the map is oriented correctly. This is the core skill of map orientation, and students learn it physically before they try it on paper.

Now ask spatial questions:
- "Which cone is closest to the red landmark?"
- "Which cones are between the yellow and green?"
- "Which cone is furthest from where we're standing?"

[SHOW: students pointing to different checkpoints as they answer]

This builds the spatial vocabulary they need for map reading.

### Segment 3: Geometric-O (15-35 min)

[SHOW: pairs receiving pattern maps and heading out to checkpoints]

Pair students up and hand out pattern maps. The progression works like this:

**Start with the triangle (3 checkpoints)**

[SHOW: pair holding a map with 3 dots connected, visiting checkpoints in order]

Each pair visits the three checkpoints in the order shown on the map. At each checkpoint, the partner checks the animal clue sheet to confirm the right one.

**Progress to harder maps**

[SHOW: pair with a more complex map showing 5-6 dots]

As pairs finish, give them maps with more checkpoints. Progress up to 10-checkpoint maps.

**Repeat for speed**

[SHOW: pair running a course they've done before, moving quickly]

Once students know the layout, have them time themselves on a course they've completed before.

**From memory**

[SHOW: pair handing their map to the teacher, then heading out]

Challenge strong pairs to complete a course without the map.

### Segment 4: Symbol-O Preview (5 min)

[SHOW: teacher showing orienteering map symbols on the whiteboard]

If time allows, introduce Symbol-O as a preview of Lesson 6. Show a few orienteering map symbols and have students find checkpoints by symbol type. This gives students a first look at how real orienteering maps work.

## Reflection Questions (visual: teacher with gathered students)

- What was challenging about matching the map to the real space?
- What sort of mistakes did you make? How did you fix them?
- How did you know the map was oriented correctly?
- Describe the mental map you built of the space

## Extensions (visual: examples of extension activities)

- Remove the colored landmark cones to make orientation harder
- **Napkin-O**: cover animal pictures with napkins. Pairs must communicate with other pairs to finish.
- **Word Bank Orienteering**: one partner reads the map, the other runs. The reader can only use approved spatial words.
- Have students draw their own map of the space
- Have students design their own course on a map

## Tips for Success (visual: return to presenter)

1. The whiteboard demonstration is the most important teaching moment in this lesson. Take your time with it. Make sure every student sees the map rotate and clicks on why orientation matters.
2. Colored landmark cones are critical for early success. Students use them to orient their maps. Remove them only after students are confident.
3. Let pairs self-pace through the progression. Some will stay on 3-checkpoint maps for most of the lesson, and that's fine. Forcing everyone to the same level leads to frustration.
